How the estimate works

Net business income is added to your other income. QPP (12.6% base and first additional, 8% second additional) and QPIP (0.764%) are calculated at self-employed rates, minus amounts already paid through employment. Quebec tax uses the 2026 brackets, the $18,952 basic personal amount and the deduction for workers (6%, max $1,450). Federal tax uses the 2026 brackets, reduced by the 16.5% Quebec abatement.

Frequently asked questions

How much QPP does a self-employed person pay in 2026?

Both shares: 12.6% of earnings between $3,500 and $74,600, plus 8% on earnings between $74,600 and $85,000. Contributions already paid through a job reduce this.

What is the QPIP rate for self-employed people in 2026?

0.764% of eligible income up to $103,000 of insurable earnings, if self-employment plus employment income is at least $2,000.

What is the Health Services Fund (FSS) contribution?

It applies to income other than employment income: 1% of the amount over $18,500 (max $150), then $150 plus 1% of the amount over $64,355 (max $1,000). This calculator estimates it in a simplified way.

When is my 2026 balance due?

Pay by April 30, 2027. Self-employed people have until June 15, 2027 to file both returns, but interest runs on unpaid balances after April 30.

Do I need to pay instalments in Quebec?

Generally yes if your net tax owing is over $1,800 for the year and for one of the two previous years (Quebec; the federal threshold for Quebec residents is also $1,800). Due dates: March 15, June 15, September 15 and December 15.

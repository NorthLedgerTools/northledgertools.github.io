California overtime in plain English

Unlike the federal rule, California counts overtime by the day as well as by the week. A non-exempt employee is owed 1.5 times the regular rate for hours over 8 and up to 12 in a workday, for hours over 40 in a workweek, and for the first 8 hours on the seventh consecutive day worked in a workweek. Double time applies to hours over 12 in a day and to hours over 8 on the seventh consecutive day. Hours already paid as daily overtime are not counted again towards the 40-hour weekly rule. The helper above applies exactly these rules to one workweek; enter the totals it gives in the pay fields.

Texas, by comparison, has no state income tax and no daily overtime: see the Texas paycheck calculator. Self-employed in the US? Use the 1099 self-employment tax calculator.

How the California income tax is figured

This follows the EDD's Method B steps. If your gross wages for the period are at or below the low-income exemption for your pay period and filing status, no tax is withheld. Otherwise, subtract the estimated-deduction amount for any additional allowances, subtract the standard deduction for the period, look up the tax in the withholding rate table for your filing status, and subtract the allowance credit. For example, the EDD's own sample of a biweekly paycheck of $1,600 for a married employee with three allowances, one of them for estimated deductions, works out to $2.38 of California tax, and this calculator returns the same figure.

One quirk to know about: the EDD's Method A wage-bracket tables are not always identical to Method B. For a biweekly paycheck for a married employee with two or more allowances, the Method A table appears to apply the single-person standard deduction ($219) instead of the $439 that Method B's Table 3 gives (which is exactly double the single amount and consistent with every other pay period). This calculator follows Method B, so for that one filing status and pay period it can differ from the Method A table by up to roughly $20 per paycheck. Your employer's payroll software may use either method.

Frequently asked questions

What comes out of a California paycheck in 2026?

Federal income tax (from your Form W-4 and the IRS Pub. 15-T tables), Social Security 6.2% up to $184,500 for the year, Medicare 1.45%, California personal income tax withholding (from your DE 4 and the EDD's 2026 schedules), and California State Disability Insurance (SDI) of 1.3% of all wages. Pre-tax items like a 401(k) or health premiums reduce some of these.

What is California SDI and is there a wage cap?

SDI is the employee-paid payroll tax that funds State Disability Insurance and Paid Family Leave benefits. The 2026 rate is 1.3% of every dollar of wages. Since January 1, 2024 there is no wage ceiling, so higher earners keep paying it all year; before 2024 it stopped at an annual wage limit.

How does California decide how much income tax to withhold?

The employer uses the DE 4 you filled in (not the federal W-4 allowances, which no longer exist) together with the EDD's withholding schedules. The exact method subtracts a standard deduction for your pay period, applies the 2026 withholding rate table for your filing status (rates from 1.1% up to 14.63%), then subtracts a credit for each regular allowance. If your pay is at or below the low-income exemption for your pay period, nothing is withheld.

What are DE 4 allowances and what are 'estimated deduction' allowances?

Regular allowances give a fixed tax credit each (for example $3.24 per allowance on a weekly paycheck in 2026). Additional allowances for estimated deductions (such as itemized deductions) reduce the wages subject to withholding by a fixed amount per allowance instead of giving a credit. Married employees with two or more regular allowances and head-of-household filers use the larger standard deduction and low-income exemption.

How is overtime paid in California?

California requires daily overtime, which is more generous than the federal weekly rule for most non-exempt employees: time-and-a-half for hours over 8 up to 12 in a day, over 40 in a workweek, and the first 8 hours on the seventh consecutive day of a workweek; double time for hours over 12 in a day and over 8 on the seventh consecutive day. The helper above applies those rules to one week of daily hours.

How are bonuses and other supplemental wages taxed?

If your employer pays a bonus or stock option separately from regular wages, California withholding is a flat 10.23% with no allowances, and federal withholding is a flat 22%. Other separate supplemental wages, such as commissions and overtime pay, are withheld at 6.6% by California. If the bonus is paid together with regular pay, it is added to the paycheck and taxed on the regular schedule instead. Social Security, Medicare (7.65%) and SDI (1.3%) apply as usual.

Does California tax 401(k) contributions on my paycheck?

Pre-tax 401(k) deferrals reduce your California income tax withholding, like federal, but they are still subject to SDI. Section 125 cafeteria plan deductions (for example health insurance premiums) are exempt from California income tax withholding and from SDI, and from Social Security and Medicare.

What is California's minimum wage in 2026?

$16.90 an hour for all employers from January 1, 2026, with higher minimums for fast food and healthcare facility employees and in many cities and counties. The Department of Finance has certified an increase to $17.40 on January 1, 2027.

Why is my pay stub different from this estimate?

Payroll systems may use the wage-bracket tables instead of the exact method (differences of a few cents), annualize irregular pay, include post-tax deductions, garnishments or local taxes, or apply a different DE 4. Pre-tax deductions beyond 401(k) and Section 125, tips, and wages above the Social Security base later in the year are not modelled. Check your pay stub.

Is overtime pay tax-free in California?

No. Overtime is wages for California withholding. A new federal deduction for the premium part of qualified overtime exists for tax years 2025 to 2028, but it is claimed on the federal return, not on your paycheck, and this page does not model any state treatment of it.

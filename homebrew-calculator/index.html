Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate ABV from gravity readings?

The standard homebrew formula is ABV = (OG − FG) × 131.25. For example, OG 1.050 and FG 1.010 gives (0.040) × 131.25 = 5.25% ABV.

How is IBU calculated?

This calculator uses the Tinseth formula: utilization = 1.65 × 0.000125^(boil gravity − 1) × (1 − e^(−0.04 × minutes)) / 4.15, and IBU = utilization × alpha acid % × hop ounces × 7490 / gallons. Boil gravity is approximated by the original gravity.

How much priming sugar do I need?

It depends on the beer volume, the highest temperature the beer reached after fermentation (which sets how much CO2 is still dissolved) and your target carbonation. The calculator uses residual CO2 = 3.0378 − 0.050062·T + 0.00026555·T² (T in °F) and 15.195 g of dextrose per US gallon per volume of CO2 to add.

How many volumes of CO2 should I target?

Typical ranges: British ales 1.5–2.0, American ales 2.2–2.7, lagers 2.4–2.7, wheat and Belgian styles 3.0–4.0. Standard bottles should not exceed about 3.0 volumes.

Can I use table sugar instead of corn sugar?

Yes. Sucrose ferments slightly more per gram, so you need about 91% of the dextrose weight. Dry malt extract needs roughly 1.47 times the dextrose weight.

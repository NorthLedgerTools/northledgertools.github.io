Overtime in Texas: the pay and the new deduction

Overtime for covered non-exempt employees is time-and-a-half over 40 hours in a workweek under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, the rule that applies to most private employers in Texas. From 2025 through 2028 the extra half-time portion can be deducted from federal taxable income, up to $12,500 ($25,000 joint), if your income is under $150,000 ($300,000 joint). The deduction is taken on your tax return, so it shows up as a smaller tax bill or larger refund rather than a bigger paycheck, unless you account for it on your Form W-4. Frequently asked questions

Does Texas have a state income tax on paychecks?

No. Article VIII, Section 24-a of the Texas Constitution says the legislature may not tax the net incomes of individuals, so no Texas income tax is withheld from your pay. Federal income tax, Social Security and Medicare still come out. Texas does charge sales and property taxes, which are not taken from paychecks.

What comes out of a Texas paycheck in 2026?

Federal income tax withholding (from your Form W-4 and the IRS Pub. 15-T tables), Social Security at 6.2% of wages up to $184,500 for the year, Medicare at 1.45% of all wages (plus 0.9% on wages above $200,000), and any pre-tax benefits you elect such as a 401(k) or health insurance. Texas unemployment tax is paid by the employer only.

How is overtime pay taxed in Texas?

Overtime is regular wages: withheld the same way, annualized by your payroll system. Because a big overtime check is annualized, it can look like a higher bracket, but your actual tax is settled on your return. Federal law requires time-and-a-half over 40 hours in a workweek for covered non-exempt employees, which applies to most private employers in Texas; this calculator uses 1.5 times your hourly rate.

Is there a tax deduction for overtime in 2026?

Yes, a new federal one. From 2025 through 2028 you can deduct the premium portion of FLSA-required overtime (the 'half' in time-and-a-half), up to $12,500 ($25,000 joint), phasing out above $150,000 of income ($300,000 joint). It is claimed on your return, so it does not change your paycheck withholding by itself; the estimate on this page shows what it could be worth.

How is a bonus taxed?

If your employer pays it separately from regular wages, federal income tax is withheld at a flat 22% (37% over $1 million of supplemental wages in a year), plus 7.65% Social Security and Medicare. In Texas there is no state tax on it. If it is paid together with regular wages, the employer may instead add it to the paycheck and use the regular tables.

How often must employers in Texas pay?

Employees who are not exempt from overtime must be paid at least twice a month, and exempt employees at least once a month, on paydays the employer designates (Texas Labor Code 61.011). Someone fired must be paid within six days; someone who quits by the next regular payday (61.014).

What is the Texas minimum wage in 2026?

$7.25 an hour, the federal minimum: Texas adopts it and does not set its own. A full-time worker at that rate earns $290 a week before tax.

Why is my paycheck different from this estimate?

Your employer's payroll system may round, use the wage-bracket tables, handle bonuses differently, or apply a different W-4 entry. Pre-tax and post-tax deductions, garnishments, union dues, tips and fringe benefits are not all modeled here. Treat it as an estimate and check your pay stub.

Should I change my W-4 if my paycheck is too small or large?

If you want a different amount withheld, adjust your Form W-4 (extra withholding in Step 4(c), or credits and deductions in Steps 3 and 4(b)). The IRS Tax Withholding Estimator is the official tool for that.

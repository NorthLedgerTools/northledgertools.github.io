How the set-aside is calculated

The calculator estimates your full 2026 bill: federal and provincial income tax plus CPP and CPP2 on self-employment earnings, minus tax already withheld from any job. Dividing that by your business profit gives the share of each dollar of profit to move into a separate savings account. It applies the 2026 federal brackets (14% to 33%) and credits, Ontario's surtax, tax reduction, LIFT credit and Health Premium, the BC tax reduction, and Alberta's 2026 brackets. Frequently asked questions

How much should a freelancer set aside for taxes in Canada?

It depends on your profit and province. As a rough guide for 2026, a single freelancer with $50,000 of profit and no other income should put aside about 22% to 23% of every dollar of profit for income tax and CPP (about 23.0% in Ontario, 22.4% in BC and Alberta). The calculator gives your exact percentage.

Why is the percentage so high at low incomes?

Self-employed people pay both halves of CPP: 11.9% of net earnings between $3,500 and $74,600. That applies from the first dollar above $3,500, before income tax even starts.

Is the set-aside based on revenue or profit?

On profit: revenue minus deductible business expenses. If you prefer to save from each payment you receive, multiply the percentage by your profit margin; the calculator also shows how much to keep from each $1,000 of profit.

Do I need to pay quarterly instalments?

If your net tax owing (income tax minus tax withheld) is over $3,000 for 2026 and was also over $3,000 in 2025 or 2024, the CRA expects instalments on March 15, June 15, September 15 and December 15. CPP on self-employment is included in the instalment amount.

When is the 2026 balance due?

April 30, 2027, even though self-employed people have until June 15, 2027 to file.

Does this include GST/HST?

No. GST/HST you collect is not your money; keep it separate from this set-aside. You must register once your taxable sales pass $30,000 over four consecutive calendar quarters.

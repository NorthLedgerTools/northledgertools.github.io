Good to know from official sources

  • "Bill 96" is the National Assembly's name for An Act respecting French, the official and common language of Québec, assented to on 1 June 2022 (chapter 14 of the Statutes of Québec, 2022). The Charter of the French language is the text to read for the rules listed here. [National Assembly of Québec, Bill 96 page]
  • The OQLF states that the Charter applies to any business or employer that carries on activities in Québec, even temporarily. [OQLF, frequently asked questions]
  • According to the OQLF's FAQ, a person who refuses to correct a breach of the Charter is liable to a fine of $700 to $7,000 for a natural person and $3,000 to $30,000 for a legal person, doubled for a first repeat offence and tripled for each additional one. The OQLF does not issue offence notices or fines: the DPCP has that power and the courts impose fines. [OQLF, frequently asked questions]
  • The OQLF's own fiche on websites says its content has no legal value; the binding text is the Charter. [OQLF fiche, social media, websites and advertising documents (2022)]
  • The OTTIAQ (the Québec order of translators, terminologists and interpreters) calls on its members to integrate automated translation tools into an OTTIAQ-approved process, which must always include at least one round of quality control by a professional, and says that whenever there are real-world consequences, a language professional should be involved. This is a professional order's position, not a legal requirement. [OTTIAQ, position on AI in translation (2024)]

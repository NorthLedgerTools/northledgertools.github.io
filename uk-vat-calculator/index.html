Prices in the UK for consumers normally show VAT-inclusive amounts, while invoices between VAT-registered businesses show the net price, the VAT and the total separately.

The registration rules, step by step

There are two separate triggers and each has its own deadline. The backward-looking test compares your taxable turnover for the previous 12 months with £90,000 at the end of every calendar month, a rolling test that does not follow the tax year. Once it is passed you have 30 days from the end of that month to apply, and HMRC treats you as VAT-registered from the first day of the second month after the month you went over. The forward-looking test applies if you can see that turnover in the next 30 days alone will exceed £90,000: you must apply by the end of that period and your effective date is the day you realised. Whichever applies first governs, and from the effective date you must charge VAT on your sales whether or not your certificate has arrived. If you only make VAT-exempt or out-of-scope supplies you do not have to register.

Once registered, a business may use the Flat Rate Scheme if it expects taxable turnover of £150,000 or less (excluding VAT) over the next 12 months. Our Flat Rate Scheme calculator shows whether it would cost you less than standard VAT accounting.

Frequently asked questions

How do I add 20% VAT to a price?

Multiply the net (ex-VAT) price by 1.2. £100 + 20% VAT = £120, of which £20 is VAT. For the 5% reduced rate multiply by 1.05; the zero rate adds nothing, but zero-rated sales still count towards your taxable turnover.

How do I take VAT out of a price that already includes it?

Divide the VAT-inclusive price by 1.2 for the standard rate to get the net price. £120 ÷ 1.2 = £100, so the VAT is £20. Equivalent shortcut: the VAT is one-sixth (the 'VAT fraction') of a price that includes 20% VAT, and one-twenty-first of a price that includes 5% VAT. Do not take 20% off the gross price: 20% of £120 is £24, which is wrong.

What are the UK VAT rates in 2026?

Standard rate 20% (most goods and services), reduced rate 5% (for example children's car seats and home energy) and zero rate 0% (for example most food and children's clothes). Some supplies are exempt from VAT, such as postage stamps and financial and property transactions, and some are outside the scope of VAT altogether.

What is the VAT registration threshold?

£90,000 of VAT-taxable turnover. You must register if your total taxable turnover over the last 12 months goes over £90,000, or if you expect it to go over £90,000 in the next 30 days alone. You can register voluntarily below the threshold. You can ask to cancel registration if your taxable turnover falls below £88,000.

How is the 12-month turnover test applied?

It is a rolling test, not a tax-year test: at the end of every month, add up your taxable turnover for the previous 12 months. If it is over £90,000, you must apply to register within 30 days of the end of that month, and your registration takes effect from the first day of the second month after you went over. For example, if you pass £90,000 during July, you must register by 30 August and the effective date is 1 September.

What is the '30-day' rule?

If you realise at any point that your taxable turnover will go over £90,000 within the next 30 days alone (for example a big contract), you must register by the end of that 30-day period and your effective date is the day you realised. If you realise on 1 May, you apply by 30 May and the effective date is 1 May.

What counts as taxable turnover?

The total value of everything you sell that is not VAT-exempt or outside the scope of VAT. It includes zero-rated, reduced-rated and standard-rated sales, goods you hire or loan to customers, business goods used for private reasons, bartered or gifted goods, and some services bought from overseas under the reverse charge. Exempt sales do not count.

What happens if I register late?

You owe VAT on sales from the date you should have registered, even if you did not charge it to customers, and you may face a penalty depending on the amount and how late you are. If you will exceed the threshold, apply on time.

Is this the right calculator for Flat Rate Scheme VAT?

No. The Flat Rate Scheme replaces output-minus-input accounting with a flat percentage of your VAT-inclusive turnover, which depends on your trade sector. Use our Flat Rate Scheme calculator to compare it with standard accounting.

Why can a total on an invoice differ by a penny?

VAT on an invoice can be worked out per line or on the invoice total, and each method rounds to the nearest penny. This calculator rounds half a penny upwards on each line and adds the lines; HMRC accepts either method if you apply it consistently, provided the VAT you show matches the VAT you account for.

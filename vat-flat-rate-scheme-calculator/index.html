VAT Flat Rate Scheme Calculator 2026: Compare with Standard VAT Accounting

Choose your trade sector and enter one VAT period of sales and purchases. The calculator applies the HMRC flat rate, checks whether you are a 16.5% limited cost business, applies the 1% first-year discount if it applies, and compares the payment with what you would owe under standard VAT accounting.

How the Flat Rate Scheme payment is worked out

Flat rate payment = flat rate percentage × VAT-inclusive turnover. VAT-inclusive turnover is the money you receive for sales that includes VAT on standard and reduced-rate sales, plus zero-rated and exempt sales. For example, a consultant with £1,000 of standard-rated sales invoices £1,200 including 20% VAT. At a sector rate of 14%, the payment to HMRC is £168 and the consultant keeps £32 of the VAT collected. Under standard accounting the payment would be £200 less whatever VAT was paid on purchases.

The limited cost business test

A business is a limited cost business if its spend on relevant goods in the period is below 2% of its VAT-inclusive flat rate turnover, or above 2% but under £1,000 a year (£250 a quarter). It then pays 16.5% regardless of sector. This calculator runs that test for the period you enter. For example, a business with £10,000 of quarterly turnover that spends £225 on relevant goods is a limited cost business, because £225 is more than 2% (£200) but under £250; the same business spending £260 pays its sector rate.

Joining and leaving

You can join if your expected taxable turnover for the next 12 months, excluding VAT, is £150,000 or less. You must leave if your VAT-inclusive turnover for the 12 months to your anniversary of joining was more than £230,000 (or you expect it to be in the next 12 months), or you expect more than £230,000 in the next 30 days alone. HMRC also lets a business that spends big on one-off capital items (over £2,000 including VAT) reclaim that VAT outside the scheme.

Frequently asked questions

How does the VAT Flat Rate Scheme work?

Instead of charging 20% to customers and reclaiming the VAT on your purchases, you still charge your customers the normal VAT and show it on your invoices, but you pay HMRC a fixed percentage of your VAT-inclusive turnover. The percentage depends on your trade sector. You keep the difference between the VAT you collected and what you pay HMRC, and generally you cannot reclaim VAT on purchases, except capital assets over £2,000 including VAT.

Who can join the Flat Rate Scheme in 2026?

You can join if you expect your VAT taxable turnover (excluding VAT) to be £150,000 or less over the next 12 months. You must leave if, on the anniversary of joining, your turnover for the last 12 months was more than £230,000 including VAT (or you expect it to be in the next 12 months), or if you expect your total income in the next 30 days alone to be more than £230,000 including VAT.

What is the limited cost business rate of 16.5%?

If you spend very little on goods you use in the business, you must use 16.5% instead of your sector rate. A limited cost business spends less than 2% of its VAT-inclusive flat rate turnover on relevant goods, or more than 2% but less than £1,000 a year (£250 a quarter). Relevant goods exclude capital expenditure, vehicles and fuel (unless you are a transport business), food and drink for you or staff, and goods bought for resale (unless that is your main business). Services do not count at all, which is why many contractors fall into it.

What is the 1% first-year discount?

If it is within the first 12 months from the date you registered for VAT, you can deduct 1 percentage point from your flat rate percentage. It also applies to the limited cost rate, so 16.5% becomes 15.5%. The discount ends on the first anniversary of VAT registration, even if you joined the scheme later.

Do exempt sales and zero-rated sales count?

Yes: the flat rate is applied to your total VAT-inclusive turnover, which includes zero-rated, reduced-rated and standard-rated sales and any exempt income. That is why the scheme is often poor value for a business with a lot of zero-rated sales, since it pays the flat rate on those sales while standard accounting would pay no output VAT on them.

How does the calculator decide if the scheme saves money?

For the period you choose it computes VAT you would pay with standard accounting (VAT you charge on 20% and 5% sales, minus VAT you can reclaim on your purchases) and compares it with the flat rate percentage multiplied by VAT-inclusive turnover. A positive difference means the scheme costs you less in that period.

Which sector rate should I use if I do several things?

HMRC says to use the percentage for your main business activity, measured by turnover. If you cannot find a sector that fits, 'Any other activity not listed elsewhere' applies. If your business changes the sector, you must update the percentage.

When do I have to use 16.5% even if my sector rate is lower?

Whenever the limited cost test is met for the return period. The test must be carried out for each return, so the same business can pay its sector rate in a quarter with equipment purchases and 16.5% in a quarter without them.

Is the scheme better for me than standard VAT accounting?

It depends on your sector rate, your sales mix and your purchases. It tends to suit businesses with few VAT-bearing costs and mostly standard-rated sales, but it often costs more for a business with high VAT-bearing costs or many zero-rated sales, or one forced into the 16.5% limited cost rate. This calculator compares a single period; check a typical quarter and a quarter with unusual spending.

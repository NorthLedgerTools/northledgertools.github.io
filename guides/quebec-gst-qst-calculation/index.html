Calculating GST and QST in Quebec: rates, method and rounding

Published 2026-10-29 · Checked against official sources 2026-10-29 · All guides

Most sales in Quebec carry two taxes. The most common mistake is charging QST on a price that already includes GST. Here is Revenu Québec's method, the rounding rule and three examples.

The two rates

Revenu Québec describes the two most common taxes this way: the goods and services tax (GST) is 5% of the price, and the Québec sales tax (QST) is 9.975% of the price excluding GST. GST and QST are collected on the sale of most goods and services. In Quebec, Revenu Québec also administers the GST/HST, under rules set by the Government of Canada.

The method in three lines

  1. Price before tax.
  2. GST = price × 5%.
  3. QST = price × 9.975% (on the same price, without adding the GST).

The total is the price plus both taxes. In Quebec, 5% and 9.975% add up to 14.975% of the pre-tax price. The combined rate is handy for checking a result, but it must not appear on an invoice (see below).

The rounding rule

According to Revenu Québec, only fractions equal to or greater than half a cent ($0.005) must be rounded to a whole cent ($0.01). If you sell more than one item, you can calculate the GST and QST on the total price of all of them before rounding. Revenu Québec also says the 9.97%, 14.97% and 14.975% rates must not appear on the document attesting to the sale.

Frequently asked questions

What is the QST rate?

9.975%, calculated on the price excluding GST, according to Revenu Québec.

Is QST calculated on the price including GST?

No. Revenu Québec says it is calculated on the price excluding GST.

How do I round GST and QST?

Only fractions equal to or greater than $0.005 must be rounded to $0.01.

Should I show 14.975% on the invoice?

No. Revenu Québec says the 9.97%, 14.97% and 14.975% rates must not appear on the document attesting to the sale.

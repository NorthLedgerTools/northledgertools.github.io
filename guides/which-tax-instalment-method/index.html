Which tax instalment method should you use? A worked comparison

Published 2026-10-25 · Checked against official sources 2026-10-25 · All guides

The CRA offers three ways to work out your quarterly instalments. They are all acceptable, but they put your cash out at different times, and instalment interest is measured against whichever method would have cost you the least.

Who has to pay instalments

You generally have to pay by instalments if your net tax owing is more than $3,000 for the current year and in either of the two previous years ($1,800 for residents of Quebec, as of December 31). Self-employed people, landlords and people with investment or pension income without enough withholding are the usual cases. The CRA mails reminders (Form INNS1) in February, for March and June, and in August, for September and December.

Payments are due March 15, June 15, September 15 and December 15.

The three methods

MethodHow it worksGood for
No-calculationPay the amounts printed on the CRA's reminder. If you only got the August reminder, pay the amount in box 2 on September 15 and December 15.Steady income; no effort
Prior-yearAdd your net tax owing from last year's return, plus CPP payable and any voluntary EI, and pay according to that figure (in the fall-start case, 75% on Sept 15 and 25% on Dec 15).Income that grew this year
Current-yearEstimate this year's net tax owing plus CPP and EI and pay that.Income that dropped this year
Worked example: a growing freelancer Aisha freelances in Ontario. Her 2025 net tax owing plus CPP was $9,200. In 2026 her net income is $60,000; the same engine behind our calculator puts her 2026 bill (income tax plus CPP) at about $14,603.
  • Prior-year method: pays about $9,200 in the year (a quarter each), then owes the remaining ≈ $5,403 when she files. No instalment interest, because she paid what the method required. The cash is due April 30, 2027.
  • Current-year method: pays $14,603 across four payments (≈ $3,651 each) and owes almost nothing at filing.
The first is easier on cash in the year and costs nothing in interest if paid on time; the second leaves nothing for April. Neither is "right": the question is whether you would rather hold the money until April, and whether you will actually keep it separate. A set-aside percentage solves the second problem.

How interest and the penalty work

Instalment interest applies if you were required to pay, got a reminder showing an amount, and paid late, not at all, or too little. It is compounded daily at the prescribed rate. The CRA compares what you paid with what you should have paid, using the payment calculation option that results in the least interest, and charges the difference if it is more than $25. That is why paying according to any of the three methods keeps you clear.

A penalty applies only if your instalment interest for the year is more than $1,000. It is half of the interest above the greater of $1,000 or 25% of the interest you would have owed with no instalments. The CRA's example: actual interest $2,500, no-payment interest $3,200: penalty = ($2,500 − $1,000) ÷ 2 = $750.

You can reduce interest by overpaying the next instalment or paying it early; the credit interest is not refundable and only offsets instalment interest for the same year.

If you live in Quebec

You deal with two governments. Revenu Québec sends its own reminder (Form TPZ-1026.A) in February and August if your net Quebec tax for 2024 was over $1,800 (August only, for the September and December payments, if only 2025 was over $1,800), and you pay it separately from the CRA, on the same four dates. Revenu Québec's $1,800 test uses net income tax, not contributions: the QPP, QPIP, Health Services Fund and drug-insurance contributions are then added to the amount you pay (its form TP-1026 divides the total by four). Revenu Québec charges interest on late instalments, plus an extra 10% a year on the shortfall if you paid less than 75% of an instalment, but no interest if you pay on time the amounts shown on your TPZ-1026.A. The CRA's threshold for Quebec residents is also $1,800. Our Quebec instalments calculator estimates each side, and the French guide acomptes provisionnels : quelle méthode covers the Revenu Québec rules.

Practical rules

  • Pay before the due date: the CRA considers an online-banking payment paid on the same or next business day, depending on your bank, so do not leave it to March 15 itself.
  • If income drops mid-year, re-run the current-year estimate: it can justify smaller later instalments. Keep your working in case the CRA asks.
  • Write down which method you chose for each year: your "least interest" comparison uses all three, and a note makes April easier.

Frequently asked questions

What are the instalment due dates?

March 15, June 15, September 15 and December 15.

What is the instalment threshold?

Net tax owing above $3,000 for the current year and either of the two previous years; for Quebec residents, $1,800.

Which instalment method has the lowest interest risk?

Any of the three avoids interest if you pay what the method requires; the CRA measures interest against the option that would have cost you the least.

Do I need to pay instalments if I did not get a reminder?

You are still responsible if you meet the threshold, but instalment interest only applies when you received a reminder showing an amount to pay.

Self-employment tax in 2026: the 15.3% that is not 15.3% of your profit

Published 2026-10-25 · Checked against official sources 2026-10-25 · All guides

Self-employment tax is the freelancer's version of FICA: 15.3% for social security and Medicare, but applied to 92.35% of your net profit, so the effective rate is about 14.1% until the $184,500 social security cap. On $60,000 of profit it is $8,477.73.

The formula

  1. Start with net profit (Schedule C line 31, or your share of partnership income).
  2. Multiply by 92.35%. This is the self-employed way of removing the "employer half" from the base, so you are taxed like an employee-plus-employer on the same dollar.
  3. Social security: 12.4% of that amount, on up to $184,500 for 2026 (the IRS's 1040-ES worksheet uses this wage base).
  4. Medicare: 2.9% of the full amount, with no cap.
  5. Add them: that is your SE tax, reported on Schedule SE. You owe it if your net earnings are $400 or more.
  6. Deduct half of it on Schedule 1 when you calculate adjusted gross income.

The social security part stops at $184,500 of the 92.35% base, which is about $199,783 of net profit. Above that you only pay Medicare (2.9%), and high earners may owe the 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax (Form 8959). The effective rate falls above the cap, but you are still paying income tax on top.

Worked example: $60,000 net profit $60,000 × 92.35% = $55,410.00. Social security: 12.4% × $55,410.00 = $6,870.84. Medicare: 2.9% × $55,410.00 = $1,606.89. SE tax: $8,477.73. The deductible half is $4,238.86, which lowers the income tax base. That is a 14.13% effective rate on the profit, not 15.3%.

What this means for planning

  • Set aside two buckets. SE tax is separate from income tax: a freelancer with $60,000 profit owes about $8,478 SE tax before any federal income tax, and may owe state tax too.
  • The deduction is not a credit. The deductible half reduces income tax by your marginal rate times $4,238.86; it does not reduce SE tax itself.
  • SE tax is included in estimated payments. See estimated tax safe harbors for how to avoid an underpayment penalty.
  • Business expenses lower SE tax because it is calculated on net profit, which is why tracking them matters.

Frequently asked questions

What is the self-employment tax rate for 2026?

15.3%: 12.4% social security on net earnings up to $184,500 plus 2.9% Medicare on all of them, applied to 92.35% of net profit.

Why multiply by 92.35%?

It removes the equivalent of the employer's half of FICA from the base, so self-employed people are not taxed on tax. The result is an effective rate of about 14.13% below the cap.

Can I deduct self-employment tax?

You deduct half of it on Schedule 1 when figuring adjusted gross income. It does not reduce the SE tax itself.

Do I owe SE tax if I earned less than $400?

SE tax applies if net earnings from self-employment are $400 or more.

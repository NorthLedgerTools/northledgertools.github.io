Self-employed CPP in 2026: you pay both halves, and it is deducted two ways

Published 2026-10-25 · Checked against official sources 2026-10-25 · All guides

If you work for yourself you pay the employee and the employer half of CPP, 11.9% of net self-employment income between $3,500 and $74,600, plus 8% on the next $10,400 (CPP2). The maximum for 2026 is $9,292.90. It is not all deducted the same way.

The 2026 rates and ceilings

Employee (each half)Self-employed paysApplies to net earnings
Base CPP5.95%11.9%$3,500 to $74,600 (maximum $8,460.90)
CPP2 (second additional)4%8%$74,600 to $85,000 (maximum $832.00)

"Net earnings" means business income after expenses. Contributions are calculated on Schedule 8 of your return, and you pay them with your income tax balance. If you also have an employer, your employer's CPP counts toward the same ceilings, so you only pay the remainder on self-employment income. Quebec residents pay QPP instead; see how we check our numbers and the Quebec calculator.

Why it is split into a deduction and a credit

Because you pay both halves, the tax system treats the employer half as a business cost and the employee half as a personal credit. On Schedule 8:

  • Line 31000 credit. The base CPP split in half: the employee-share of the base contribution gets a non-refundable credit at the lowest federal rate (14% for 2026).
  • Line 22200 deduction. The other half of the base CPP plus all of the enhanced (first additional) and CPP2 contributions is deducted from your income.
Worked example at $50,000 net Total CPP is $5,533.50. Of that, base CPP is 9.9% × $46,500 = $4,603.50 and the enhanced part is 2% × $46,500 = $930.00. The credit base is half of base CPP: $2,301.75 (line 31000). The deduction is the other $2,301.75 plus $930.00 = $3,231.75 (line 22200). The income tax saving from the deduction depends on your marginal rate; the credit saves 14% of $2,301.75 federally, about $322.

Planning points

  • It is a flat cost, not a bracket. Whether you earn $30,000 or $74,600 you pay 11.9% on the part above $3,500, so the first dollar above the exemption costs 11.9 cents in CPP before any income tax.
  • CPP stops mattering above $85,000. Beyond the CPP2 ceiling your marginal rate no longer includes CPP, which is why set-aside percentages fall as income rises.
  • It builds a pension. Unlike income tax, CPP contributions raise your future retirement benefit, which is worth remembering when you see the total.
  • Include it in instalments. The CRA's instalment calculations add CPP payable to net tax owing, so CPP is part of what you pay quarterly. See which instalment method to use.

Frequently asked questions

How much CPP do self-employed people pay in 2026?

11.9% of net self-employment income between $3,500 and $74,600 (up to $8,460.90), plus 8% of income from $74,600 to $85,000 for CPP2 (up to $832.00).

Is self-employed CPP tax deductible?

Partly in each way: half of the base contribution is a credit (line 31000), and the other half of the base plus all enhanced and CPP2 contributions are a deduction (line 22200).

Do Quebec self-employed workers pay CPP?

No. They pay the Quebec Pension Plan, at 12.6% up to $74,600 and 8% on the next $10,400, with a maximum of $9,791.

Do I still pay CPP if I have a job and a side business?

Contributions from both sources count toward the same yearly maximum. If your employer already took the maximum, you do not owe more on the business income.

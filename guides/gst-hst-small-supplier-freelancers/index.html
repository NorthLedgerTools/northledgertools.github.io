The $30,000 GST/HST small supplier rule for freelancers

Published 2026-10-25 · Checked against official sources 2026-10-25 · All guides

You can skip GST/HST registration while you are a small supplier: no more than $30,000 of taxable revenue in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters. The tricky part is that the test is rolling, and the day you cross it you must charge tax on the sale that crosses it.

The two tests

For most businesses, the CRA looks at your total revenue (before expenses) from worldwide taxable supplies, including zero-rated supplies and those of associated persons. It excludes financial services, sales of capital property and goodwill. A calendar quarter runs January–March, April–June, July–September or October–December.

If you exceed $30,000...You stop being a small supplier...You must...
in a single calendar quarteron the day of the sale that takes you overcharge GST/HST on that sale, and register within 29 days
over the previous four consecutive quarters (not in one quarter)at the end of the month after the quarter in which you exceedcharge from the first sale after that, and register within 29 days
Worked example A designer's revenue by quarter: Q1 $4,000, Q2 $9,000, Q3 $11,000, Q4 $8,000: total $32,000 over the four quarters, but no single quarter above $30,000. She becomes a non-small supplier at the end of the month after Q4 (end of January). She charges tax from her first sale after that date.

If she lands one $35,000 contract in a quarter, she crosses the line on the day of that sale, and she must charge GST/HST on that contract itself.

What it means in practice

  • Watch two numbers, not one. Track revenue per calendar quarter and a running four-quarter total. Our self-employed calculator deals with income tax; the GST/HST test is separate and uses gross revenue, not profit.
  • You cannot recover tax you did not charge. If you cross the line and fail to charge, you owe the GST/HST yourself.
  • Registering early can make sense. Voluntary registration lets you claim input tax credits on business purchases. Your effective date is usually the day you ask (or up to 30 days earlier). The trade-off is that you must charge clients and file returns.

Exceptions

  • Taxi and commercial ride-sharing drivers must register and charge GST/HST from their first fare, even when under $30,000.
  • Non-residents selling cross-border digital services or hosting certain events follow different registration rules.
  • Quebec: you register for GST and QST with Revenu Québec, and the small-supplier rules apply to both taxes. See the Revenu Québec page below.

After you register

Charge the tax on invoices, keep receipts for input tax credits, and file returns on the schedule your filing period sets. The tax you collect is not your income: the set-aside percentage for income tax and CPP is separate, so keep GST/HST money in its own account.

Frequently asked questions

When do I have to register for GST/HST as a freelancer?

When your taxable revenue exceeds $30,000 in a single calendar quarter, or over four consecutive quarters. You register within 29 days of the effective date.

Is the $30,000 profit or revenue?

Revenue before expenses from taxable supplies, not profit.

Do I have to charge GST/HST on the sale that takes me over $30,000?

Yes, if you pass the limit in a single quarter. Your registration is effective no later than the day of that sale.

Do Uber drivers need to register?

Yes. Commercial ride-sharing and taxi drivers must register from their first fare regardless of the $30,000 limit.

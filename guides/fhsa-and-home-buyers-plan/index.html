FHSA and the Home Buyers' Plan: can you use both?

Published 2026-10-25 · Checked against official sources 2026-10-25 · All guides

You can use an FHSA qualifying withdrawal and an RRSP Home Buyers' Plan withdrawal for the same home, if you meet each program's conditions at the time of each withdrawal. They behave very differently afterwards: FHSA money is never repaid; HBP money must go back into your RRSP over 15 years.

How FHSA room builds up

Participation room is $8,000 for the year you open your first FHSA. Unused room carries forward, but the carry-forward is capped at $8,000, so the most you can add in one year is $16,000. Lifetime is $40,000. Your room is reduced by RRSP-to-FHSA transfers, and a taxable withdrawal does not restore it unless the CRA's re-participation rules apply.

What a qualifying FHSA withdrawal needs

All of these, per the CRA:

  • You are a first-time buyer for the withdrawal: you did not live in a home you owned (or jointly owned) as your main residence in the current year before the withdrawal (not counting the 30 days just before) or the previous four calendar years.
  • You have a written agreement to buy or build a qualifying home in Canada, with the acquisition or completion date before October 1 of the year after the withdrawal.
  • You did not acquire the home more than 30 days before the withdrawal.
  • You are a Canadian resident from the first withdrawal until you acquire the home.
  • You intend to occupy it as your main home within one year of buying or building it.
  • You give your issuer Form RC725.

If any condition fails, the amount is a taxable withdrawal and is added to your income for that year. There is no minimum holding period for contributions before a qualifying withdrawal. Once you make a qualifying withdrawal, close the FHSA by December 31 of the following year.

How the Home Buyers' Plan works

An eligible first-time buyer can withdraw up to $60,000 from RRSPs without it being taxed, then repay it over 15 years. Each year the minimum is your balance divided by the years left. Repay nothing and that year's minimum is added to your income. Repay more and later minimums shrink.

Check the start date of repayment. The CRA's repayment page grants a temporary three-year deferral only to people whose first HBP withdrawal was made between January 1, 2022 and December 31, 2025 (repayments start in the fifth year after the withdrawal). For earlier withdrawals the repayments start the second year after. We read the CRA's page as not extending the relief to 2026 withdrawals; confirm your own start year on your HBP statement or with the CRA before you plan around it.

To repay, you contribute to an RRSP in the repayment year or the first 60 days after, then designate it as a repayment on Schedule 7. You cannot repay an HBP into an FHSA. And the CRA warns that RRSP contributions made in the 89 days before an HBP withdrawal may not be deductible; plan the timing.

Using both for the same home

The CRA confirms that you can make an HBP withdrawal and a qualifying FHSA withdrawal for the same qualifying home, as long as you meet all conditions at the time of each. Couples can each use their own FHSA and HBP. A sensible order is to use FHSA first (no repayment), and then the HBP only for the amount you need, since each dollar of HBP creates a 15-year repayment schedule.

Traps to avoid

  • Excess FHSA amounts cost 1% a month on the highest excess, and a qualifying withdrawal does not clear one.
  • Moving FHSA money to a TFSA is a taxable withdrawal plus a TFSA contribution: only direct transfers to an RRSP, RRIF or another FHSA avoid tax.
  • Re-contributing a qualifying withdrawal is a new, non-deductible contribution.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use the FHSA and the Home Buyers' Plan together?

Yes. The CRA says you can use both for the same qualifying home as long as you meet the conditions at the time of each withdrawal.

How much can I take out under the HBP?

Up to $60,000 from your RRSPs, repaid over 15 years.

Do I have to repay an FHSA qualifying withdrawal?

No. Unlike the HBP, qualifying FHSA withdrawals are not repaid.

What is the FHSA carry-forward limit?

Unused participation room carries forward up to $8,000, so the most you can contribute in a year is $16,000. The lifetime limit is $40,000.

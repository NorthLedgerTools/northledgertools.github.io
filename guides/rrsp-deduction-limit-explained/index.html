RRSP deduction limit explained: 18%, the cap and the $2,000 buffer

Published 2026-10-25 · Checked against official sources 2026-10-25 · All guides

Your RRSP room for 2026 is 18% of your 2025 earned income up to $33,810, plus unused room from earlier years, minus pension adjustments. The 2027 maximum is already set at $35,390. And the penalty only starts once you are more than $2,000 over.

How your RRSP room is built

Your RRSP deduction limit for a year is: 18% of the previous year's earned income (up to the dollar limit), plus any unused room carried forward, minus your pension adjustment, plus any pension adjustment reversal. Your notice of assessment shows the exact figure for the next contribution year.

Two points often confused: the room is based on last year's earned income, so your 2025 earnings create room used for the 2026 tax year; and "earned income" is mainly employment income and net self-employment income, not investment income or most capital gains.

The deadline

Contributions made in the first 60 days of a year can be deducted on the prior year's return. For 2026 the deadline is March 1, 2027. A contribution after that date counts for 2027.

Worked example: inside the buffer and past it

Priya's numbers 2025 earned income: $72,000. Unused room carried forward: $4,000. No pension adjustment.
2026 room: $4,000 + 18% × $72,000 ($12,960) = $16,960.
  • She contributes $18,500 by March 1, 2027: $1,540 over. Because the CRA allows a lifetime $2,000 cushion, no tax applies, but she cannot deduct the extra $1,540 until she earns more room.
  • She contributes $21,000: $4,040 over. The cushion covers $2,000, leaving $2,040 over it, so the 1% tax is $20.40 a month (about $245 over a year) until she withdraws the excess or new room absorbs it.

Report the tax on Form T1-OVP; the CRA's deadline is 90 days after the end of the year the excess relates to (March 31, 2027 for 2026). Withdrawing the excess promptly stops further months of tax.

The $2,000 buffer is cumulative: it applies to your total over-contributions over time, not each year. And over-contributions inside the buffer still produce no deduction, so they save no tax; they just earn tax-sheltered growth. That is a reason not to aim for it.

Why this matters for your refund

A deduction is worth your marginal rate. Use the RRSP refund calculator to see the saving at your income and province, then check that the contribution fits inside your room first. Contribute above room and you get no refund on the excess.

Special case: HBP and FHSA

Repayments under the Home Buyers' Plan do not reduce your RRSP deduction limit, and direct transfers from an FHSA to an RRSP generally do not affect RRSP room. See FHSA and the Home Buyers' Plan.

Frequently asked questions

What is the RRSP limit for 2026?

$33,810, reached with 2025 earned income of about $187,833. The 2027 limit is $35,390.

What is the RRSP contribution deadline for the 2026 tax year?

March 1, 2027.

Can I over-contribute to my RRSP without penalty?

Up to a cumulative $2,000 over your limit, with no tax. Beyond that, the tax is 1% per month on the excess above the cushion. Over-contributions within the buffer cannot be deducted.

Where do I find my RRSP deduction limit?

On your latest notice of assessment and in your CRA account, which shows the limit for the current year.

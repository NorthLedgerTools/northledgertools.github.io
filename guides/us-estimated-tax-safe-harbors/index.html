Estimated tax safe harbors: how little can you pay and still avoid a penalty?

Published 2026-10-25 · Checked against official sources 2026-10-25 · All guides

You can avoid the IRS underpayment penalty without predicting this year's income exactly: pay at least the smaller of 90% of this year's tax or 100% of last year's tax (110% if last year's AGI was above $150,000). Withholding counts too.

Who has to pay estimated tax

For 2026, the IRS says you generally must pay estimated tax if you expect to owe at least $1,000 after withholding and refundable credits, and you expect withholding and credits to be less than the smaller of 90% of 2026 tax or 100% of your 2025 tax (your 2025 return must cover all 12 months). If your 2025 AGI was above $150,000 ($75,000 if married filing separately), use 110% instead of 100%.

2026 due dates

PaymentDue date
1stApril 15, 2026
2ndJune 15, 2026
3rdSeptember 15, 2026
4thJanuary 15, 2027*

* You do not have to make the January payment if you file your 2026 return by February 1, 2027 and pay the balance in full. Note the quarters are not equal calendar quarters: the 2nd payment covers two months, and the 3rd covers three.

A: paying the prior-year amount ($18,000) is the cheaper safe harbor, so the freelancer pays $4,500 per payment and owes the other $6,000 when filing, with no underpayment penalty. B: with AGI above $150,000 the prior-year route is 110% of last year's tax, $19,800. It is still below 90% of this year's tax ($21,600), so it is the safe harbor: $4,950 per payment. C: income fell, so 90% of this year's tax ($21,600) is smaller than last year's $30,000.

Choosing the approach

  • Prior-year method: certainty. You know the number in January and you never have to estimate. Best when income is rising.
  • 90% of current year: lower cash out when income has fallen, but you need a decent estimate.
  • Annualized income: if income is lumpy (a big Q4), Form 2210 Schedule AI lets you match payments to when you earned it.
  • Withholding: withholding from a job counts as if paid evenly through the year, which helps when you also freelance.

Include self-employment tax

Estimated tax covers income tax and self-employment tax (explained here). The 1040-ES worksheet estimates both. A set-aside rule of thumb based on your actual numbers is better than a flat percentage; the 1099 calculator gives a quarterly figure.

State and what to do next

This page covers federal rules. Many states have their own estimated tax rules and dates, which are often the same four dates but not always. Check your state's department of revenue.

Frequently asked questions

How much estimated tax do I have to pay to avoid a penalty?

The smaller of 90% of your 2026 tax or 100% of your 2025 tax (110% if your 2025 AGI was more than $150,000), counting any withholding.

When are 2026 estimated tax payments due?

April 15, June 15 and September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027.

Do I need to make the January 15 payment?

Not if you file your 2026 return by February 1, 2027 and pay the full balance with it.

Do I need to pay estimated tax if I will owe less than $1,000?

Generally no. The requirement applies when you expect to owe at least $1,000 after withholding and refundable credits.
